Blackball Pool Rules
Objective

Pot all seven balls of your group (reds or yellows), then pot the black to win the frame.

Racking and breaking
  • The winner of the lag decides who breaks. Players then take turns to break in each frame.
  • The cue ball can be placed anywhere in baulk for the break.
Legal and illegal breaks
  • Potting the cue ball on the break is a foul, and the cue ball is then played from baulk.
  • If the black is potted on the break, the balls are re-racked and the same player breaks again.
  • The table is always still open after the break, whatever was potted. Groups are never decided on the break.
Open table and choosing groups
  • While the table is open, you can hit a red or a yellow first. You cannot play the black first unless you have a free shot.
  • Once you pot balls from only one group, that group is yours for the rest of the frame.
  • The table stays open if the pot comes from the break, from a shot that is a foul, from a free shot, or from a combination shot that pots both a red and a yellow.
  • Groups are never called or nominated. They are decided only by what is potted.
A legal shot

The cue ball must hit one of your "on" balls first, and then either:

  • pot one of your "on" balls, or
  • send the cue ball or any other ball into a cushion.

If nothing is potted and no ball hits a cushion after contact, it is a foul. This means you cannot simply roll up behind a ball to leave a snooker: a ball still has to reach a cushion.

Snookers
  • You are snookered when no straight line from the cue ball reaches any part of an "on" ball.
  • Before playing out of a snooker, get your opponent (or the referee) to agree that it is a full snooker.
  • When escaping a snooker, you only need to hit an "on" ball. No ball has to reach a cushion afterwards.
Touching balls
  • If the cue ball is touching an object ball, you must play away from it. If the touching ball moves, it is a foul.
  • If the touching ball is one of yours (or is otherwise "on"), it counts as already hit, so you only need to pot a ball or reach a cushion.
  • If the touching ball is not "on", you must still hit an "on" ball first as well.
Combination shots
  • You can pot two or more balls in one shot, in any order and in any pockets, including balls from both groups. The first ball the cue ball hits must still be one you can legally hit.
  • You can pot your last group ball and the black in the same shot to win the frame, even in the same pocket.
Fouls and free shots

It is a foul if you:

  • Hit a ball that is not "on" first.
  • Pot nothing and do not get a ball to a cushion after contact (unless you are escaping a snooker).
  • Pot the cue ball.
  • Move a ball you are touching when playing away from it.
  • Pot only your opponent's ball, even after hitting your own first.

After a foul, your opponent gets a free shot:

  • The table is treated as open for that shot, so they can hit any object ball first, including the black.
  • Any ball they pot counts. Potting balls on a free shot does not decide groups.
  • They can play the cue ball from where it lies, or move it anywhere in baulk. If the cue ball was potted, it is played from baulk.
Balls off the table
  • Any ball that leaves the table goes back onto it. If it is the cue ball, it is played from baulk.
  • Object balls are re-spotted on the black spot, or as close to it as possible, in a straight line towards the nearest end cushion without touching each other.
  • The black goes back first, then any balls belonging to the player about to play. On an open table, the order is black, then red, then yellow.
Losing the frame

You lose the frame if you:

  • Pot the black while you still have balls of your own group on the table at the end of the shot.
  • Make no real attempt to hit an "on" ball, or deliberately play a ball that is not "on". The referee or your opponent decides whether it was deliberate. Otherwise it is a standard foul.
Stalemate

If no legal shot is possible, whether by accident or on purpose, the frame is re-racked. If there is any path to an "on" ball, even an unlikely one, it is not a stalemate and you must try to play it.
